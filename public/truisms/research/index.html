  • D01.1ReviewRace formats, rules and logistics

    Standard is hard. 70.3 is patient.

    The mechanism

    A standard-distance race is driven by high carbohydrate use and hard muscle recruitment. A 70.3 is governed by thrift: sparing glycogen and keeping the gut working.

    Numbers on the card

    • 88-92% vs 75-82% FTP
    • Standard: threshold
    • 70.3: save glycogen

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Specific aspects of contemporary triathlon: implications for physiological analysis and performance

    Bentley DJ, Millet GP, Vleck VE, McNaughton LR

    Sports Medicine 2002; 32(6): 345-359

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of what makes triathlon different from its three sports on their own: wetsuits, drafting, the change from bike to run, and the rules of elite and age-group races.

    Check before you rely on it

    The draft cited this paper under a different title. The FTP percentages are not in the paper, and they do not match the ranges in the app (BIKE_IF in pace.js).

    On race day

    Hold an Olympic bike near the top of your sustainable range. Hold a 70.3 bike lower, so you can still run. Burning matches early costs the most on the half marathon.

  • D01.2RulesRace formats, rules and logistics

    The draft zone is a box, not a suggestion.

    The mechanism

    Wind tunnel and computer models show that a rider 12 m behind another at 40 km/h still gets a 10-14% cut in drag, about 15-22 W. Strict box rules keep the race fair.

    Numbers on the card

    • 12 m draft box
    • 25 s pass window
    • 5 min penalty

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Blocken B, van Druenen T, Toparlar Y, et al. Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics 2018; 179: 319-337, and World Triathlon Competition Rules, section 5.3.

    An engineering journal, which PubMed does not index, so it was not checked. The rulebook was not checked either.

    What the study did

    Not known. The draft says computer models and wind tunnel tests with mannequins.

    Check before you rely on it

    The box length, pass time and penalty are not checked against the current rules. They differ by race and age group. Read your own race briefing.

    On race day

    Enter the box only to pass, and finish the pass inside the time allowed. If someone passes you, drop back out of the box before you pass again.

  • D02.1Source not foundSwim

    Straight is faster than strong.

    The mechanism

    Swimmers who drift off line add distance. Sighting every few strokes costs far less than correcting a long detour.

    Numbers on the card

    • +50-120 m on poor sighting
    • Sighting penalty
    • +30 to 90 s lost

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Chollet D, Hue O, Keskinen KL. International Journal of Sports Medicine 2000; 21(1): 54-60.

    That page range holds a different paper, on arm coordination in front crawl, not sighting. No source was found for the 4.2 degree drift or the extra distance.

    What the study did

    Not known.

    Check before you rely on it

    No source found for any figure on this note.

    On race day

    Sight every 6 to 8 strokes. Lift only your eyes as you breathe, so your hips stay up.

  • D02.2Source not foundSwim

    Drag costs more than power.

    The mechanism

    Most of a swimmer’s effort goes into overcoming drag, and the body turns only a small share of it into speed. Cutting drag gains more than pulling harder.

    Numbers on the card

    • 90% lost to drag
    • 10-15% efficiency
    • Water: 800x air

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Toussaint HM, Truijens M. Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise 2005; 37(10): 1622-1631.

    That page range holds a paper on resistance training. No source was found for the 90% figure.

    What the study did

    Not known. The draft says active drag was measured with the MAD system.

    Check before you rely on it

    No source found for any figure on this note.

    On race day

    Keep a long body line, a neutral head and a narrow kick before you add more arm turnover.

  • D03.1BookBike

    Train to zones, not to feel. Sweetspot is the workhorse.

    The mechanism

    Riding at 88-93% of FTP builds the aerobic engine with less nervous system stress and muscle damage than harder intervals.

    Numbers on the card

    • Sweetspot: 88-93% FTP
    • Mitochondrial gains
    • 45-90 min in zone

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Coggan AR, Allen H. Training and Racing with a Power Meter. VeloPress, 2010.

    A book, so PubMed does not hold it. The PubMed link in the draft was dropped: nothing shows it leads to this book.

    What the study did

    Not known. The draft says lactate curve analysis and power meter data from competitive riders.

    Check before you rely on it

    A coaching book has not been through journal review. No source found for the physiology claims.

    On race day

    Build up to 45-90 minutes at sweetspot a week, in blocks such as 2 x 20, 3 x 15 or 1 x 45 minutes, in base and build.

  • D03.3Source not foundBike

    Your race number is lower than you think. Smooth power is cheap power.

    The mechanism

    Drag climbs steeply with speed, so surging uphill saves seconds while easing off downhill loses minutes. Big swings burn glycogen early and cost you on the run.

    Numbers on the card

    • VI under 1.05
    • Standard: 88-92%
    • 70.3: 76-82% FTP

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Suriano R, Bishop D. Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport 2010; 13(4): 456-461.

    PubMed has no paper matching this author, journal, year and pages.

    What the study did

    Not known. The draft says 16 triathletes did variable and steady cycling, then a 10 km run.

    Check before you rely on it

    No source found. The percentages also differ from the ranges in the app (BIKE_IF in pace.js).

    On race day

    Compare normalised power with average power. Keep the variability index (normalised power divided by average power) under 1.05 for a 70.3.

  • D04.1Lab studyRun

    Cadence beats foot strike. Check your form when tired.

    The mechanism

    A faster step rate at the same speed shortens your stride and lands your foot nearer your centre of mass. That cuts the energy your knee and hip absorb at each landing.

    Numbers on the card

    • 172-182 spm
    • Less knee load
    • +5-10% step rate

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Effects of step rate manipulation on joint mechanics during running

    Heiderscheit BC, Chumanov ES, Michalski MP, Wille CM, Ryan MB

    Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 2011; 43(2): 296-302

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    45 healthy recreational runners ran on a treadmill at a steady speed at their own step rate and at 5% and 10% above and below it, while motion and force were recorded.

    Check before you rely on it

    The paper found less knee energy absorbed at +5% and +10%, and less hip energy at +10%. The 172-182 spm window and the 20-34% figure are not in its abstract. The draft gave a different title and DOI.

    On race day

    When your legs feel heavy off the bike, lift your step rate by 3 to 5 steps a minute to get your spring back.

  • D04.2ReviewRun

    Threshold lifts race pace. Space your hard runs.

    The mechanism

    Work at the lactate threshold trains your muscles to clear lactate, which lifts the pace you can hold without tiring.

    Numbers on the card

    • Threshold pace
    • 48-72 h between hard runs
    • 10k-half pace

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Lactate threshold concepts: how valid are they?

    Faude O, Kindermann W, Meyer T

    Sports Medicine 2009; 39(6): 469-490

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of 25 ways to define a lactate threshold. 32 studies compared them with endurance performance, and most found strong links, especially in running.

    Check before you rely on it

    The draft gave a different title and described it as a review of 80 or more trials. The 48-72 hour gap and the session sizes are not in the abstract.

    On race day

    Do 20 to 30 minutes of threshold work in total, for example 3 x 8-10 minutes, and keep it 48 to 72 hours away from hard rides.

  • D06.1ReviewStrength, Mobility and Injury

    Lift heavy, not long. Strength makes you economical.

    The mechanism

    Heavy lifting stiffens tendons and helps your muscles fire together, so the same speed costs less oxygen.

    Numbers on the card

    • 80-85% 1RM, 4-6 reps
    • Running economy
    • Tendon stiffness

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Optimizing strength training for running and cycling endurance performance: A review

    Rønnestad BR, Mujika I

    Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports 2014; 24(4): 603-612

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of studies that added heavy or explosive strength training to the training of endurance-trained runners and cyclists.

    Check before you rely on it

    The review reports better running economy with heavy or explosive work, and recommends heavy work for cycling economy. The 4-8% figure and the load range are not in its abstract.

    On race day

    Lift twice a week: 3 to 4 sets of 4 to 6 reps of squats, trap bar deadlifts and calf raises.

  • D07.1ReviewFuelling and Hydration

    Count grams, not gels. Eat the most on the bike.

    The mechanism

    Running jolts the gut and sends blood to the legs. Sitting on the bike keeps the gut working, so the bike is the best place to take in fuel.

    Numbers on the card

    • 70% on the bike
    • 60-90 g/h carbs
    • Standard: 40-60 g/h

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    A step towards personalized sports nutrition: carbohydrate intake during exercise

    Jeukendrup A

    Sports Medicine 2014; 44 (Suppl 1): S25-S33

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of recent carbohydrate research that sets advice by how long you exercise and by the type of carbohydrate.

    Check before you rely on it

    The review says one sugar is used at up to about 60 g an hour, and about 90 g an hour needs more than one sugar. It does not give the 70% bike share or the blood flow figure. Planned carbs in this app never pass the gut figure you enter.

    On race day

    Take most of your carbohydrate on the bike. On the run, keep to gels and drinks that sit easily.

  • D07.2Crossover trialFuelling and Hydration

    Mix your sugars. Too concentrated means trouble.

    The mechanism

    Glucose and fructose use different gut transporters. Together they let the body use more carbohydrate than glucose alone, without a gut full of sugar.

    Numbers on the card

    • Ratio 2:1 to 1:0.8
    • SGLT1 + GLUT5
    • 6-8% drink

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Oxidation of combined ingestion of glucose and fructose during exercise

    Jentjens RLPG, Moseley L, Waring RH, Harding LK, Jeukendrup AE

    Journal of Applied Physiology 2004; 96(4): 1277-1284

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    Eight trained cyclists rode 120 minutes at 50% of maximum power on four visits, drinking glucose alone, more glucose, glucose with fructose, or water. Labelled sugars showed how much each person burned.

    Check before you rely on it

    The trial used glucose and fructose at 2:1 and found a peak of about 1.26 g a minute. The 1:0.8 ratio and the 6-8% strength are not from this paper. The draft gave 2005 and different page numbers.

    On race day

    Choose a drink or gel with glucose and fructose, at about 2:1 or 1:0.8, and mix bottles at 6 to 8% carbohydrate.

  • D07.3Position standFuelling and Hydration

    Know your sweat rate. More water is not always better.

    The mechanism

    Too little fluid raises your heart rate and body heat. Too much plain water can dilute the sodium in your blood.

    Numbers on the card

    • 500-1000 mg sodium
    • 500-800 ml/h
    • Under 2% body weight lost

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    American College of Sports Medicine position stand. Exercise and fluid replacement

    Sawka MN, Burke LM, Eichner ER, Maughan RJ, Montain SJ, Stachenfeld NS

    Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 2007; 39(2): 377-390

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A consensus statement from the American College of Sports Medicine on how to keep hydration right during activity.

    Check before you rely on it

    The stand says to avoid losing more than 2% of body weight to water, to measure your own sweat rate by weighing before and after, and to use drinks with salt and carbohydrate when needed. The sodium and fluid ranges here are not in its abstract. Planned fluid in this app never passes your sweat rate.

    On race day

    Weigh yourself before and after an hour at race pace to find your sweat loss. Plan fluid and sodium around that, never above it.

  • D08.3Closest matchPacing and race strategy

    Run the first 5 km easier than you want. Think in thirds.

    The mechanism

    Adrenaline off the bike hides how hard you are working. Starting the run too fast burns glycogen and heat early, then you slow in the final third.

    Numbers on the card

    • 1st third: controlled
    • First 5 km
    • +5 to 10 s/km slower

    Source

    Closest match in PubMed

    Pacing strategies during the swim, cycle and run disciplines of sprint, Olympic and half-Ironman triathlons

    Wu SSX, Peiffer JJ, Brisswalter J, Nosaka K, Lau WY, Abbiss CR

    European Journal of Applied Physiology 2015; 115(5): 1147-1154

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    Named in the draft: Wu SSX, Peiffer JJ, Brisswalter J, Nosaka K. Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport 2014; 17(5): 522-526.

    The paper the draft names was not found. This is the closest paper by the same authors.

    What the study did

    Eight trained male triathletes raced sprint, Olympic and half-Ironman distances within two months. Cycle power, speed and heart rate were logged, and GPS tracked the swim and run.

    Check before you rely on it

    This paper found a positive run pacing strategy (slowing) in the Olympic and half-Ironman races. The draft’s study of 120 age-group athletes was not found. The 5-10 s/km figure is not in this paper.

    On race day

    Split the 21.1 km run into thirds. Keep the first 7 km easy and conversational, settle into goal pace for the next 7 km, then race the last 7 km.

  • D10.5ReviewPeriodisation, load and taper

    Taper flatness is normal. Longer race, longer taper.

    The mechanism

    Cutting training volume while keeping intensity lets the body shed fatigue and keep its fitness, which lifts performance on race day.

    Numbers on the card

    • Volume cut
    • Keep intensity
    • About 3% gain

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Scientific bases for precompetition tapering strategies

    Mujika I, Padilla S

    Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 2003; 35(7): 1182-1187

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of taper research and the changes it brings in the body and the mind.

    Check before you rely on it

    The abstract says to cut volume by up to 60-90%, keep intensity, cut frequency by no more than 20%, and expect a gain of about 3% (usual range 0.5-6%). It puts the best taper length between 4 and more than 28 days. The draft said 40-60% over 8-14 days and called the paper a meta-analysis; the app’s own taper guide is 14 days for a 70.3. The draft gave a different title and DOI.

    On race day

    Cut hours sharply in the last days, keep short efforts at race pace, and keep your usual number of sessions.

  • D11.3ReviewRecovery and health

    Above the neck, maybe. Below it, no. Heat adaptation takes two weeks.

    The mechanism

    Heat acclimation expands blood plasma, improves sweating and lowers body temperature, so the same effort feels easier in the heat.

    Numbers on the card

    • 10-14 days
    • Plasma volume up
    • Hot water baths

    Source

    Found in PubMed

    Adaptations and mechanisms of human heat acclimation: Applications for competitive athletes and sports

    Périard JD, Racinais S, Sawka MN

    Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports 2015; 25 (Suppl 1): 20-38

    Open the paper on doi.org (another site) ↗

    What the study did

    A review of heat acclimation regimens, the changes they cause, and how athletes can use them.

    Check before you rely on it

    The review says the size of the gain depends on the intensity, length, frequency and number of heat exposures. The 10-14 day figure and the plasma numbers are not in its abstract.

    On race day

    Do 60 to 90 minutes of easy training in warm layers, or a 20 to 30 minute hot bath after training, on 10 to 14 days in a row before a hot race.

  • D12.2Source not foundEquipment and fit

    Tyres are the cheapest speed. Wider and softer can be faster.

    The mechanism

    On real roads, very high tyre pressure wastes energy bouncing you up and down. A supple wider tyre at a lower pressure can roll faster.

    Numbers on the card

    • 10-16 W saved
    • 1-2 min over 90 km
    • 60-75 psi

    Source

    Not found in PubMed

    Named in the draft: Grappe F, Candau R, Belli A, Rouillon JD. International Journal of Sports Medicine 1999; 20(8): 526-531.

    PubMed has no paper at that page range. A 1999 paper by these authors on cycling resistance (Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 31(10): 1441-1447) tests a way to measure drag and rolling resistance. It does not test tyre pressure.

    What the study did

    Not known.

    Check before you rely on it

    No source found for the watts, the time saved or the pressure range.

    On race day

    Fit good race tyres with latex or tubeless set-up, and test lower pressures than you use now.
